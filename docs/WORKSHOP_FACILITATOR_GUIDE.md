# Workshop Facilitator Guide

A ready-to-run 90-minute community session. Print this page and the two forms at the end.

---

## Before you go

**Equipment**
- Laptop with CyberSathi running (`make run`) — **works without internet**
- Projector or a large screen; if neither, 2–3 phones passed around works
- Printed consent forms and handouts (templates at the end of this document)
- Printed certificates, or a printer afterwards

**Setup check, 10 minutes before**
1. Start the backend and open `http://127.0.0.1:8000/docs` to confirm it responds.
2. Create the workshop in the app and add participants as they arrive.
3. Have `/scam/examples` open — never type a real scam message live on stage.
4. Write **1930** on the board before anyone sits down. Leave it there the whole session.

**Tone.** The single most important thing you do is remove shame. People who have been cheated
stay silent, and silence is what lets the same scam run through a whole village. Say early and
plainly: *doctors, engineers and bank managers have all fallen for these. It is not stupidity —
these are professionally designed.*

---

## Session plan — 90 minutes

### 0–10 min · Welcome and consent

- Introduce yourself and the college/department.
- Explain what the session covers and that no personal data will be collected beyond a name and
  age group, with phone numbers stored only as a one-way code.
- Distribute and collect consent forms.
- **Ask one question to open the room:** *"Has anyone here — or someone in your family — ever
  received a message asking for an OTP?"* Almost every hand goes up. That is your entry point.

### 10–20 min · Pre-test

- Open **Assessment → Pre-test** for each participant, or hand out the printed version.
- 10 questions, one per screen, no time pressure.
- **Say clearly: this is not an exam, and nobody will see your individual score.** You are
  measuring whether the session works, not whether they do.
- Seniors may need help reading. Pair each with a student volunteer.

### 20–55 min · Awareness campaign (the core)

> **Use the in-app checklist.** After the pre-test, tap **Start awareness campaign**. It lists
> every topic with the ones this participant got wrong pulled to the top and marked *Priority*,
> each linking to the article to teach from. Tick topics as you cover them, or print the list.

Cover the eight categories. Spend longest on OTP, UPI and digital arrest — those cause the most
loss. For each: tell the story first, then show the tool.

| Minutes | Topic | Live demo |
|---|---|---|
| 20–26 | **OTP scams** | Paste a sample into the Scam Analyzer, show the score and the "why" |
| 26–32 | **KYC / bank messages** | Show the URL Checker on `sbi-kyc-update-verify.xyz` |
| 32–38 | **UPI & QR** | The golden rule (below) — biggest impact of the whole session |
| 38–43 | **Job & investment** | "A real employer pays you; you never pay them" |
| 43–48 | **Digital arrest** | "This does not exist in Indian law" — say it twice |
| 48–52 | **Loan apps & impersonation** | Permissions demo: why a loan app wants your contacts |
| 52–55 | **Social media & blackmail** | Emphasise: do not pay, do not delete, report |

**The one sentence they must leave with:**

> **Scanning a QR code or entering your UPI PIN is ALWAYS to PAY.
> You never scan, and never enter a PIN, to RECEIVE money.**
>
> **QR ஸ்கேன் செய்வதும் UPI PIN போடுவதும் எப்போதும் பணம் செலுத்தவே.
> பணம் பெற ஒருபோதும் ஸ்கேன் செய்யவோ PIN போடவோ தேவையில்லை.**

Write it on the board. Ask the room to repeat it. Come back to it twice more before the end.

**Demo technique.** Do not lecture at the tool. Ask the room to decide first: *"Is this message
real or fake? Hands up for fake."* Then run the analyzer and let the screen settle the argument.
People remember what they predicted.

### 55–70 min · QR / UPI scenario game

Open **QR & UPI Safety → Scam Scenario Game**. Ten situations, three choices each.

- Read the situation aloud, in Tamil if that is the room's language.
- Take a vote by show of hands *before* revealing.
- Discuss why the wrong answer is tempting — that is the actual lesson.
- Best scenarios for a mixed room: *refund QR*, *marketplace advance*, *fuel pump wrong amount*,
  *screen share*.

This is the highest-energy part of the session. Do not rush it.

### 70–80 min · Post-test

- Same process as the pre-test. The app automatically serves **different questions covering the
  same topics**, so it measures learning rather than memory of the paper.
- Reassure again that individual scores are private.

### 80–90 min · Helpline drill and certificates

**The 1930 role-play.** Knowing the number is not the same as being able to use it while
panicking. Run this properly:

1. Ask a volunteer to act out: *"I shared my OTP ten minutes ago and money is gone."*
2. Walk through aloud, together:
   - Call **1930** immediately — the first hour matters most
   - Report at **cybercrime.gov.in**
   - Call the bank and block the card
   - **Do not delete** the SMS, the call log or anything else — it is evidence
   - Tell one family member
3. Have the whole room say the number out loud together, twice.

Then show each participant their improvement screen, hand out certificates, and collect feedback.

**Close by asking each person to teach one thing to one person at home this week.** That is where
the real reach of the project comes from.

## Adapting to your audience

| Audience | What to change |
|---|---|
| **School students** | Lead with social media, gaming and account theft. Use the scenario game early — attention goes fast. Warn about being used as a "money mule" for a fee. |
| **Senior citizens** | Slow down. Turn on **"Explain like I'm 60"** mode and increase the font size. Prioritise OTP, digital arrest and fake customer care. Repeat the helpline more often. Pair each with a volunteer. |
| **Rural / low literacy** | Run entirely in Tamil. Lean on the "Read aloud" button. Use the printed poster more than the screen. Focus on UPI, loan apps and job scams. |
| **Women's SHG** | Emphasise loan-app harassment and blackmail, and that both are crimes where they are the victim. Mention the confidential reporting category. |
| **College students** | Job and task fraud, investment groups, and the money-mule warning: letting someone use your bank account for a commission is a criminal offence. |

---

## Handling difficult moments

**Someone discloses they were cheated.** This will happen. Do not make them an example. Thank
them for speaking, state plainly that it is not their fault, and offer to help them file at 1930
and cybercrime.gov.in after the session. Ask if you may share the story anonymously; only use it
if they agree.

**Someone asks you to recover their money.** You cannot, and neither can the app. Say so
directly. Help them report it. Warn them explicitly that anyone who offers to recover money for
a fee is running a second scam that specifically targets people who have already been cheated.

**Someone argues that the padlock/https means a site is safe.** Good — that is a teachable
moment. Free certificates mean most phishing sites now have the padlock too.

---

## After the session

1. Confirm every participant has both a pre-test and a post-test recorded — pairs are what the
   impact analysis needs.
2. Export the data: **Dashboard → Impact report (PDF)** for the appendix, or **CSV** for analysis.
3. Collect session feedback in the app: **Workshops → How was the session?**
4. Note attendance, photographs (with consent) and any testimonials for the report.
5. Add anything new you heard to the knowledge base — local scam variants are valuable.

## Printable: One-page handout

> ### 🛡️ Seven rules that protect your money
> ### 🛡️ உங்கள் பணத்தைக் காக்கும் ஏழு விதிகள்
>
> **1. Never share an OTP. Ever.**
> No bank, no officer, no company will ask for it.
> **OTP-ஐ ஒருபோதும் யாரிடமும் சொல்லாதீர்கள்.**
>
> **2. You never scan a QR or enter a PIN to RECEIVE money.**
> A PIN always sends money OUT.
> **பணம் பெற QR ஸ்கேன் செய்யவோ PIN போடவோ தேவையில்லை.**
>
> **3. A real employer pays you. You never pay them.**
> Any registration or security fee is a scam.
> **உண்மையான முதலாளி உங்களுக்குப் பணம் தருவார். நீங்கள் தர வேண்டியதில்லை.**
>
> **4. "Guaranteed returns" do not exist.**
> Promising fixed profit is illegal for registered advisers.
> **"உறுதியான லாபம்" என்பது கிடையாது.**
>
> **5. "Digital arrest" does not exist in Indian law.**
> No officer questions you on a video call. Disconnect and tell your family.
> **"டிஜிட்டல் கைது" இந்திய சட்டத்தில் கிடையாது.**
>
> **6. Never install a screen-sharing app.**
> AnyDesk, TeamViewer — these hand over your phone.
> **திரை பகிர்வு செயலியை ஒருபோதும் நிறுவாதீர்கள்.**
>
> **7. Urgency means stop.**
> Every scam rushes you. Pause and ask one family member.
> **அவசரம் என்றால் நிறுத்துங்கள். ஒருவரிடம் கேளுங்கள்.**
>
> ---
> ### ☎️ If it has already happened
> **Call 1930 immediately** — the first hour matters most
> **Report at cybercrime.gov.in**
> **Block the card. Do NOT delete any messages — they are evidence.**
>
> **உடனே 1930-ஐ அழையுங்கள். cybercrime.gov.in-ல் புகார் அளியுங்கள்.**
> **செய்திகளை அழிக்காதீர்கள் — அவை ஆதாரம்.**
>
> *It is not your fault. Report it — that is how it stops for the next person.*
> *இது உங்கள் தவறு அல்ல. புகார் அளியுங்கள்.*
